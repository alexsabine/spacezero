The Invitation

AI is being built in the noise. We are offering the ground.

Eighty-five percent of people do not trust artificial intelligence, and do not believe it is being built for them. That distrust is not a failure of engineering. It is a failure of ground — of the conditions, attention and care from which good things are made.

Space Zero sits six hours' drive north of San Francisco, where the redwoods meet the Pacific, deliberately away from the velocity of the city. This is the flagship build-out of Lab Zero, our practice of bringing lived experience and scientific curiosity into the same room. We propose to take three of the most significant minds in their fields each season — a scientist, an artist, a philosopher — and hold them here long enough to think differently.

Not a conference. Not a sprint. A residency, in nature, with time and care, answering provocations that matter, and producing something the public can see, hold and use.

The Provocations

Alignment as ecological care

Each trio is given a provocation to live inside for their season. They are not abstract. They treat the alignment of intelligence and the flourishing of the planet as a single question — because they are one.

01

What does an intelligence that is aligned with life actually feel like from the inside?

Alignment is usually framed as control. We ask the trio to render it instead as attunement — the way a body, an ecosystem or a community knows what it owes the whole. What would it mean to build that, not bolt it on?

02

If the planet could speak to the people building AI, what would it ask of them?

The compute behind frontier AI carries a planetary cost. We ask the trio to make that relationship visible and felt — to translate ecological stakes into something a technologist cannot look away from.

03

Where do frontier technology and ancient wisdom actually touch — and what is made there?

Contemplative traditions have studied mind, attention and interdependence for millennia. We ask the trio to find the genuine point of contact with machine intelligence, and to build something real on it.

04

How do we return wonder — and trust — to a public that has been given neither?

The work must reach beyond the gallery. We ask each trio to produce an artefact of genuine utility and aesthetic quality, in the name of scientific democratisation: science made open, beautiful and shared.

How a Season Works

Three people. One ground. One question.

Each season convenes a single interdisciplinary trio, held end-to-end by the Space Zero team. The rhythm follows the coast and the calendar — arrival, immersion, making, and the seasonal exhibition that opens the work to the community.

One  scientist One  artist One  philosopher or wisdom teacher
Arrive

Held on arrival

Travel, accommodation and a working subsidy are provided. The team grounds each resident in the place, the practice and — where appropriate and welcomed — the indigenous community of this coast.

Immerse

Think differently

Daily embodied practice, deep conversation and somatic sharing loosen the priors each resident arrives with. The provocation moves from idea to lived enquiry.

Make

Build something real

The trio produces a work of functional utility and aesthetic quality. Throughout, they are filmed and interviewed — the process itself becomes an open resource.

Exhibit

Open the work

Each season culminates in exhibition. Three seasons gather, once a year, into the annual Emergent Exhibition — a moment of collective seeing.

What Gets Made

Useful. Beautiful. Public.

Every residency leaves three things behind — and the technologists, machine-learning researchers and decision-makers we convene are brought into embodied contact with all of them.

The Artefact

A made thing of real utility and aesthetic quality — environmentally aware, awareness-raising — that carries the season's enquiry into the world and stands on its own.

The Record

Filmed and interviewed throughout, each trio's process becomes an open multimedia resource — distributed freely in the name of scientific democratisation.

The Exhibition

A seasonal show, gathered yearly into the Emergent Exhibition, where AI specialists and tech leaders meet the work in person — embodied, not on a screen.

Why Crescent City

The distance is the point.

The people shaping artificial intelligence work at the centre of enormous noise. Space Zero is built at the opposite pole — held by the beauty of the Northern California coast, where redwoods meet the Pacific and beauty itself becomes a working condition. It sits six hours' drive north of San Francisco.

To attune to the nature of intelligence, a person must first attune to their own. The remoteness of this place is not an inconvenience to be apologised for. It is the instrument. Here, those who build with technology can become embodied in a natural aesthetic, and from that ground, build differently.

Residents are not left to fend for themselves. They are held — logistically, pastorally, and creatively — by a team whose whole practice is the art of holding.

Why Imagine, Why Now

Putting humanity and the planet at the core

Imagine exists to put technology, humanity and the planet at the centre — and to build the human and emotional resilience the people behind technology need to do good work. Space Zero is the ground where that happens. We see this not as a grant spent, but as ecosystem infrastructure: an investment in the inner conditions of the people who build the future.

People, planet, profit

Our provocations treat aligned intelligence and a flourishing planet as one question. The work raises awareness of the critical issues facing the planet, and returns trust to a public that has lost it.

Resilient builders

Imagine invests in founders' mental and emotional resilience. Space Zero is where the people building AI become embodied, attuned and resourced — the inner work that better building depends on.

A node in the ecosystem

Space Zero connects labs, universities and communities — Hum Lab, True North, county and city — into a living exchange. This is the kind of human-centred ecosystem Imagine was built to grow.

More voices at the table

Artists, contemplatives and indigenous communities are brought into direct contact with frontier technology. Inclusion is not a value statement here; it is the method.
